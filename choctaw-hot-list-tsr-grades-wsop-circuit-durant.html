The Choctaw Hot List: TSR Grades the WSOP Circuit Durant Schedule

The WSOP Circuit is in Durant Oct 28 through Nov 9. We ran all 43 events through the TSR Score. Here are the 15 that graded C+ or better, what the grades mean, and where the structures play faster, so you can pick your spots.

By Shomari Williams · October 28, 2026

Forty-three events. Thirteen days. One drive up 75 to Durant. Nobody plays them all, so we did the homework. We ran every one of the 43 Choctaw structure sheets through the TSR Score. The 15 on this list are the events that graded C+ or better. The other 28 are the $130 Nightlies, the Mega satellites and the three $400 one-day NLH events (#13, #35 and #42), and we cover the fast ones below.

The top of the board

#34 Main Event: $1,700, A+. The Main has the highest TSR Score on the schedule and the lowest fee share of any of the 43 events. Your $1,700 breaks down to $1,515 for the prize pool and $185 in admin fees, so 10.9% of your buy-in is fee. The next-lowest is the Mini Main at 11.4%; every $400 event is 17.5%. You start with 40,000 chips, which is 200 big blinds at the first level (100/200 with a 200 big-blind ante). Levels run 40 minutes, then go to 60 minutes at level 18. You get two re-entries per flight, and registration stays open until the start of level 13. Flight A is Friday, Nov 6, and Flight B is Saturday, Nov 7, both at 11 AM. It's scheduled as a 3-day event, with Day 2 on Sunday, Nov 8 and Day 3 on Monday, Nov 9. The operator's structure sheet doesn't list a guarantee, so we aren't claiming one.

What does the score mean? The TSR Score measures structure only: how many minutes of play your starting stack covers before the blinds catch it, divided by how fast the blinds grow, minus a penalty for any big blind jump. It isn't a percentage and it isn't capped. On the curve the Main is graded on, an A starts at 85. The Main scored 108.0. No other event on that curve scored higher than 76.9 (the Monster Stack, the #4 Freezeout and the #17 and #39 Double Stacks).

#12 Ladies: $300, A+. The top grade on our under-$400 curve. You get 30,000 chips (150 big blinds) with 30-minute levels, and late registration runs until the start of level 9. It's a one-day event, Sunday, Nov 1 at 10 AM.

Why some events are on a different curve

TSR grades on three curves so events are compared with their peers. Under TSR Methodology v1.1, single-day series events under $400 use Curve 2. Series events at $400 or more, and any multi-day series event, use Curve 3.

That's why the two $300 Seniors events are graded differently:

  • #12 Ladies and #41 Seniors One Day are $300 and scheduled as one-day events, so they're on Curve 2.
  • #16 Seniors ($50K GTD) is also $300, but it's scheduled as a 2-day event, with a restart Tuesday at noon. Multi-day events go on Curve 3.

Bottom line: compare letter grades within the same curve. #12's A+ and #41's B are on Curve 2. Every other grade on this list is on Curve 3.

The tiers

  • A+: #34 Main Event ($1,700) and #12 Ladies ($300, Curve 2).
  • B: #1 Monster Stack ($400, 40k), #4 Double Stack Freeze Out ($500, 40k), #16 Seniors $50K GTD ($300, 30k), #17 NLH Double Stack ($400, 40k), #29 $200,000 GTD Mini Main ($1,100, 30k; guarantee per the operator's schedule), #39 NLH Double Stack ($400, 40k) and #41 Seniors One Day ($300, 30k, Curve 2).
  • B−: #14 Big O ($400, 25k), #20 40/40/40 ($400, 40k), #24 40/40 For All ($400, 40k) and #25 PLO ($400, 25k).
  • C+: #5 $250K Gargantuan ($400, 20k) and #21 NLH ($400, 20k).

Faster Structures

Not everybody wants a two-day grind. If you'd rather get in, gamble and get out, here's where the clock moves faster. These numbers come straight from the operator's level sheets.

  • The Mega satellites ($200 to the Main, $130 to the Mini Main): 5,000 chips, which is 50 big blinds at 100/100 with a 100 ante. Levels are 15 minutes, with unlimited re-entry and late reg until the start of level 5, about an hour in. The Main Event Megas award one Main seat for every $1,700 in the prize pool. They're built to get you a seat cheap, not to play deep.
  • The $130 Nightlies: 20,000 chips (100 big blinds) with 20-minute levels. Unlimited re-entry, late reg until the start of level 7, which is two hours of play. One-day events.
  • The $400 one-day NLH events (#13, #35 and The Finale, #42): 25,000 chips (125 big blinds) with 20-minute levels and late reg until the start of level 9. These graded D+ and didn't make the list, but they're a full one-day tournament.
  • #41 Seniors One Day: 30,000 chips with 20-minute levels, so it moves faster than the 30-minute #12 Ladies and #16 Seniors, even with the same stack. 50 and over only.
  • #5 Gargantuan and #21 NLH: both use the same 30-minute levels as the Monster Stack and the Double Stacks, and both go to 45-minute levels at level 25. The difference is the start: 20,000 chips, or 100 big blinds, against 200 big blinds in the 40k events. That's why they graded C+. Both have unlimited re-entry and late reg until the start of level 9, so you can fire again if you bust early. The Gargantuan's $250K guarantee and multiple flights make it the big-field play of the opening week.

For comparison, the 40/40 events (#20 and #24) give you 40,000 chips with 40-minute levels the whole way, and the Main gives you 40-minute levels that go to 60.
